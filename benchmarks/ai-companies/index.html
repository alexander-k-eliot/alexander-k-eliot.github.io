We Scored AI Companies' Own Websites for AI-Readiness

The same 9-point machine-readability audit we run on SaaS, e-commerce, dental, and financial-advisory sites, pointed at the AI industry's own homepages on 2026-07-22. If anyone should have this solved, it's the companies building the agents.

30% of the 10 AI companies we tried blocked our fetcher outright: openai.com, perplexity.ai, x.ai. All three returned HTTP 403 to a clearly identified, respectful, single-request bot -- the same kind of access their own products need to read anyone else's site.

The irony, stated plainly

Three of the ten companies we checked, all of them building products whose entire premise is an AI agent reading the web on a user's behalf, block that exact kind of automated, respectful fetch from reaching their own homepage. This isn't a security failure. It's a defensible business decision made by companies who, in the same breath, ask everyone else's website to be more open to exactly this kind of request. We aren't naming a villain here. We're naming the gap between what an industry sells and what its own front door does.

Methodology

Six server-side requests per site (the public homepage plus robots.txt, sitemap.xml, llms.txt, llms-full.txt, agents.md), a clearly identifying User-Agent, 9 weighted 9 machine-readability checks (llms.txt, llms-full.txt, agents.md, AI-crawler access in robots.txt, structured data, sitemap, title and meta description, no-JS legibility, a machine-findable contact path), public data only, dated 2026-07-22. Sites returning a non-200 response (403, timeout, or similar) are reported as blocked, not scored as zero -- same honest-scope rule as every benchmark on this site. Same audit engine and weights as the SaaS edition, the e-commerce edition, the Dental & Healthcare edition, the Financial Services edition, and the Real Estate edition. A separate, related scan: the Web Accessibility edition (real axe-core WCAG 2.2 scan, not this AI-visibility method). A companion track: we also ran a real axe-core WCAG 2.2 scan of these same 10 companies' homepages. See the AI Industry accessibility edition, a separate, non-comparable rubric.
