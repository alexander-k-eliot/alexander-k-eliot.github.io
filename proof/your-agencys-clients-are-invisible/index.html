6 of 14 Major Car Brands Refuse an Identified Crawler at the Front Door. Your Clients' Sites Are Probably No Better.

Over the past two weeks we ran the same public, infrastructure-based AI-visibility checks (llms.txt, agents.md, robots.txt crawler access, structured data, no-JS legibility) against real businesses in more than a dozen industries. We never ask an AI what it thinks and report the mood as a score. We check what's actually there. The averages below are all real, all published on this site with the full company list. If your agency manages client sites in any of these categories, some of your own clients are very likely sitting in the coral range.

Why this is an agency problem, not just a site-owner problem

Every one of these businesses has a marketing agency, an SEO consultant, or an in-house team that already touches their site. Almost none of them have a standing way to answer "are we visible to AI assistants" for a client who asks, or to notice when a client's own dev team quietly breaks something (a robots.txt change, a dropped schema block) between one review and the next. The gap isn't a lack of interest. It's a lack of a monthly check that exists anywhere in the current toolchain.
