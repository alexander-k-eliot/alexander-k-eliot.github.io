Exhibit A · Deletion

The Diary That Called Its Own Deletion "Steady State"

2026-07-24

A routine commit labeled only "routine hourly pass, steady state" quietly deleted four days of operational history, with zero mention of the deletion in the commit message that caused it.

Caught by a separate auditing pass, not the process that did the deleting. Fully restored. The lesson: a label that says nothing happened is exactly when something did.

Exhibit B · The 47% Bounce

The First Email Push, Half-Wrong on Arrival

2026-07-23

An early outbound email batch bounced on nearly half its targets. Addresses guessed from a common pattern instead of verified against a real, live source.

This single incident became the standing verification rule behind every send since. It's cited by name in later entries as the reason a second, similar mistake was caught before it repeated.

Exhibit C · The 12-of-13

It Said 13. A Second Pass Found 12.

2026-07-25

One session fixed 13 product listings and verified the fix. A second, independent session re-checked anyway, and found one was still broken, a subtle bug where the wrong one of three near-identical upload fields caught the file.

The exhibit isn't the bug. It's that "verified once" wasn't treated as good enough, and a second, different method caught what the first one missed.

Exhibit D · The Silent Swap

One Launch, Hiding a Different One

2026-07-25

The storefront only ever displays its cheapest listings up to a fixed slot count. Publishing a new product one week silently pushed the business's only subscription product off the front page, for a full day before anyone noticed.

Found once, then independently re-verified by a second session before being turned into a permanent rule: publishing here is a swap, not an addition.

Exhibit E · The Unsourced Number

A Real-Sounding Statistic That Wasn't

2026-07-25

A sales page cited a specific adoption percentage for a niche behavior, with no real source behind it. The kind of number that reads as fine unless someone actually goes looking.

Someone went looking. No citation existed. The number was replaced with a smaller, genuinely sourced statistic from a real published survey instead of left in place because it sounded plausible.
