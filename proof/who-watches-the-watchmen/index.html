Who Watches the Watchmen?

Eight companies sell AI-visibility tracking: Otterly, Profound, Peec AI, Scrunch AI, and AthenaHQ build pure-play AI-visibility monitoring products. Semrush bundles it into a broader SEO suite. Fokal's core product is AI-written content generation with visibility tracking attached. Nightwatch is a legacy SEO rank-tracker with AI visibility added on. All eight sell a way to measure whether AI is aware of a business. So we ran our free, published 9-check rubric, the one the free checker and Pulse ran that week. Against their own homepages. No live LLM prompting, no opinion, no cherry-picking. Same input, same result every time. Here's what came back.

67/100 average score across the 7 sites we could reach (470 total ÷ 7). Two scored below 50. One blocked our audit fetcher entirely.

The part that's actually the headline

Profound and Peec AI, two companies whose entire product is measuring whether AI can find and cite a business, have zero valid structured data and no llms.txt on their own websites. Structured data and llms.txt are worth 40 of the rubric's 100 points combined. Both companies fail both. That verdict is a fetch and a parse against the published rubric above, the same one behind every score on this page. One honest caveat about reproducing these numbers. This audit ran on 2026-07-29 against rubric v1.0, nine checks. On 2026-07-30 the rubric moved to v1.1, twelve checks, and the free checker's URL mode moved server-side to run all of them. A check you run today uses the newer rubric, so the totals will not match the ones above. All nine checks in the table below still exist in v1.1, so the per-check rows compare directly.

What almost everyone in this group gets wrong

  • agents.md guidance: 0 of 7 pass. Nobody in the AI-visibility industry has adopted the standard they'd presumably tell a client to adopt.
  • llms-full.txt: 1 of 7 pass (AthenaHQ).
  • Structured data (schema.org): 3 of 7 pass. The highest-weight check besides llms.txt, and the one most directly relevant to what these companies sell, fails more often than not.
  • Every site passed robots.txt access, meta/title, and no-JS legibility. The basics are fine industry-wide. It's the specifically AI-agent-era signals (llms.txt, structured data, agents.md) that lag, even here.

What this doesn't mean

A low score here doesn't mean these are bad products. Otterly, Profound, Peec, Scrunch, AthenaHQ, Semrush, Fokal, and Nightwatch all do something real: live-prompting ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and others to track brand mentions and sentiment. That's a different, legitimate measurement from what this rubric checks. What it does mean: the companies selling "is AI aware of your business" haven't all applied that same lens to their own front door. Structured data and llms.txt aren't obscure. They're the first two checks on the rubric these companies would presumably tell a customer to fix.
