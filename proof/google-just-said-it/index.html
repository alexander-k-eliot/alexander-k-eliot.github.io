Google made it official: you don't need llms.txt for Google. Here's what we do with the file.

A lot of "AI SEO" is being sold on a file most AI systems never read. If you paid for one, this page is for you. If you were about to, read it first.

On June 15, 2026, Google updated its AI-optimization guidance to say it plainly: you don't need to create new machine-readable files, AI text files, markup, or Markdown to appear in Google Search or its generative AI features. Google Search doesn't use them. Gary Illyes had already compared llms.txt to the long-dead keywords meta tag in 2025. Now it's in the official documentation.

The independent data says the same thing. Ahrefs tracked 137,210 domains that publish an llms.txt file: 97% of those files received zero requests in May 2026. SE Ranking tested 300,000 domains with a machine-learning model and found that removing llms.txt as a variable made citation prediction more accurate. The file is noise, not signal.

What actually moves whether AI systems can read and cite you

Four things, in evidence order:

01Crawler access

A robots.txt that doesn't silently block the retrieval bots. The single most common failure we find.

02Structured data that resolves to you

Real Organization/LocalBusiness JSON-LD, not boilerplate.

03Extractable answers

Content a non-JS fetch can actually read. In our 2026-07-31 re-measure of 231 real business sites, a major insurer's homepage served 0 characters of readable text without JavaScript, and gymshark.com served 14. Raw data is public in benchmark-data.

04Third-party trust corpus

Reviews, directories, consistent entity facts.

The first three are exactly what our free audit checks, the same way, every time. The fourth, reviews and directories and consistent entity facts, is real but is not something a single-page fetch can score. Our checker doesn't measure it, and we say so instead of pretending.
