AI Visibility Tools in 2026: An Honest Comparison

A round-up published elsewhere this month ranked six AI-visibility tools: Fokal, Profound, Otterly.ai, Peec AI, Nightwatch, and Scrunch AI. All six are real, all six do something useful, and five of the six cost $99/mo or more, or don't publish a price at all (Fokal is the exception, with a free tier). Here's what each one actually does, and where a free, infrastructure-based checker fits in a category none of them cover.

All six are real companies doing real work in this space. Nothing above is a knock on any of them, it's what each one's own site and the roundup that ranked them say about what they do.

What all six have in common

Every one of them measures AI visibility by asking an LLM questions and reading the answers back, whether that's Profound's "Prompt Volumes," Peec's "Position" metric, or Otterly's "AI Prompt Research." That's a legitimate, useful measurement. It's also inherently a snapshot: ask the same question tomorrow, or with a different sampling seed, and the answer moves. None of the six check whether the underlying infrastructure, the actual files and markup a crawler reads, is even in place to be found at all.

What we check instead

The free AI Visibility Checker this studio runs never calls an LLM to ask its opinion. It checks twelve technical conditions directly: a file exists or it doesn't, a schema block validates or it doesn't, a robots.txt entry blocks a crawler or it doesn't. Same input, same result, every time, no live AI query anywhere in the pipeline. Full detail: GEO Is Not Guesswork.

This isn't a substitute for what the six tools above do. It answers an earlier, more basic question: can an AI agent even read your site at all, before anyone measures whether it chooses to mention you. If the answer is no, "$99/mo to track mentions" is measuring a number that infrastructure gaps are already suppressing.
